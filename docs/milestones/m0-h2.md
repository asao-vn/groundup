# M0: H2 from the ground up

**Status:** passed (groundup 0.0.1, 2026-11-04).

M0 is the smallest complete joint: an exact quantum calculation, a rule derived
from it by code, a classical simulation that runs on that rule only, and a
comparison with experiment.

```
layer 0   quantum     exact Schrödinger solution for H2 (full CI)
   │                  → potential energy curve; no empirical input
bridge    derive      Morse bond rule extracted from that curve
   │                  → with provenance and residuals
layer 1   classical   two atoms obeying Newton's law + the derived rule
   │
validate              predictions vs NIST experimental data
```

## Layer 0: exact quantum mechanics

Within the Born–Oppenheimer approximation the electrons are solved for at
fixed nuclear positions, and the resulting energy as a function of bond length
is the potential energy curve on which the nuclei move
([Born & Oppenheimer 1927](../references.md#born1927)).

For each of 15 bond lengths between 1.0 and 6.0 bohr, groundup computes:

1. a Hartree–Fock reference, then
2. **full configuration interaction (FCI)**, which is the exact solution of the
   electronic Schrödinger equation within the chosen basis set
   ([Szabo & Ostlund 1996](../references.md#szabo1996);
   [Helgaker et al. 2000](../references.md#helgaker2000)).

The basis set is cc-pVTZ ([Dunning 1989](../references.md#dunning1989)), and the
solver is PySCF ([Sun et al. 2018](../references.md#sun2018);
[Sun et al. 2020](../references.md#sun2020)). The dissociation limit is computed
the same way, as twice the energy of an isolated H atom. With one electron,
Hartree–Fock is already exact within the basis.

No experimental number enters layer 0.

## Bridge: deriving the bond rule

Layer 1 needs a rule for the force between two atoms. groundup derives a Morse
potential ([Morse 1929](../references.md#morse1929)):

```
V(r) = E∞ − De + De · (1 − exp(−a (r − re)))²
```

Each of its parameters comes from a quantity measured on the layer-0 curve:

| Parameter | Taken from |
|---|---|
| `re` | minimum of a degree-6 polynomial fitted to the points within 0.4 bohr of the lowest sampled energy |
| `De` | exact dissociation limit (2 × E(H)) minus the energy at that minimum |
| `a` | curvature `k` at the minimum: `a = sqrt(k / (2 De))` |

The rule's provenance records its source method, inputs, and its residual
against every layer-0 point. For FCI/cc-pVTZ the Morse rule reproduces the
curve with an RMS residual of 628 cm⁻¹ (maximum 1623 cm⁻¹). That residual is the
information this joint loses.

The Morse oscillator also has closed-form quantum levels
([Morse 1929](../references.md#morse1929)). In atomic units, with reduced mass μ:

```
ω_e     = a · sqrt(2 De / μ)
ω_e x_e = ω_e² / (4 De)
G(v)    = ω_e (v + ½) − ω_e x_e (v + ½)²
D_0     = De − G(0)
```

## Layer 1: classical atoms

Two hydrogen atoms (mass 1.00782503223 u each) are placed on a line, displaced
0.01 bohr from `re`, and released. They move under Newton's second law and the
derived rule, integrated with velocity Verlet
([Swope et al. 1982](../references.md#swope1982)) for 30,000 steps of 1 atomic time
unit (about 0.024 fs). Layer 1 knows nothing about electrons.

The vibration frequency is measured from the times at which the bond length
crosses its mid-line. Total energy is conserved to 1 × 10⁻⁴ of the vibrational
energy.

## Validation

Experimental values are from the NIST Chemistry WebBook
([NIST / Huber & Herzberg](../references.md#nist-h2)).

| | Check | groundup | Experiment | Error | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PASS | bond length r_e | 0.7426 Å | 0.7414 Å | +0.16% | ±1% |
| PASS | harmonic frequency ω_e | 4411.1 cm⁻¹ | 4401.2 cm⁻¹ | +0.22% | ±2% |
| PASS | dissociation energy D_0 | 4.430 eV | 4.478 eV | −1.06% | ±2% |
| PASS | fundamental G(1) − G(0) | 4154.4 cm⁻¹ | 4158.5 cm⁻¹ | −0.10% | ±2% |
| PASS | layer-1 MD frequency vs layer-0 ω_e | 4410.9 cm⁻¹ | 4411.1 cm⁻¹ | −0.00% | ±0.2% |
| INFO | anharmonicity ω_e x_e | 128.3 cm⁻¹ | 121.3 cm⁻¹ | +5.8% | recorded loss |
| INFO | classical MD frequency vs fundamental | 4410.9 cm⁻¹ | 4158.5 cm⁻¹ | +6.1% | recorded loss |

The experimental fundamental is computed from NIST's tabulated constants as
ω_e − 2ω_e x_e.

## What this joint loses

- **Shape.** A three-parameter Morse curve only approximates the true potential,
  so the anharmonicity is 5.8% too large.
- **Quantum nuclei.** Classical atoms have no zero-point motion. They vibrate at
  the harmonic frequency ω_e, not at the measured fundamental, a 6.1% difference.
  The derived rule still predicts the fundamental to 0.1% once it is quantized.

## Known limitations

- Most of the −1.06% error in D_0 is expected to come from the finite cc-pVTZ
  basis. A larger basis has not been run yet.
- Relativistic, adiabatic and non-Born–Oppenheimer corrections are ignored.
  They are small for H2 but nonzero.
- Exact FCI is affordable only because H2 has two electrons. It will not scale
  ([Concepts](../concepts.md#why-not-simulate-a-cell-atom-by-atom)).

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -e '.[dev]'
.venv/bin/python -m groundup m0 --out results/m0_h2.json
```

The run takes about 15 seconds on a laptop. The JSON output contains every
layer-0 energy, the derived rule with its full provenance, the layer-1 settings,
and every check.
